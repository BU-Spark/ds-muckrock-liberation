Storyline 0 (opening): Requests nearly tripled since FY2008, mostly at immigration-related units¶

The later storylines all separate DHS, or four immigration-related units, before drawing conclusions. This one explains why, and answers the project's first base question: how have federal FOIA request and processing volumes changed, and how does that vary by agency?

The finding in one sentence (Section 6): government-wide FOIA requests received rose from 560,978 in FY2008 to 1,707,413 in FY2025, 3.04 times as many (2.86 times the FY2010 level, when the list of agencies is nearly complete); 74.3% of the increase is at four immigration-related units, USCIS, CBP, ICE and EOIR, whose share of all requests rose from 19.7% to 58.7% in FY2024. Requests at other units (without VBA and DHS's other components) were roughly flat for a decade, fell to a low in FY2020–22, recovered by FY2024, and were the highest of the 18 years only in FY2025.

Terms:

  • Received = requests received in the year (Table 01; handbook Section V counts perfected and non-perfected requests); processed = requests completed in the year (Table 01). Both are counts of requests; one requester can file many.
  • Perfected = a request that meets the requirements and can be processed; the statutory clock starts then. Tables 16–18 (handbook Section VII) count perfected requests only.
  • The four units are USCIS, CBP and ICE (in DHS) and EOIR (in DOJ), a grouping of units, not of request topics.
  • DHS's other components = every DHS component except USCIS, CBP and ICE, including PRIV, CISA, FEMA, TSA and USCG.
  • An agency is a department or independent agency; a component is a unit within an agency that reports separately.
  • Abbreviations: DHS (Homeland Security; its USCIS, CBP, ICE, PRIV, the Privacy Office, and CISA, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency); DOJ (Justice; its EOIR, the immigration courts); VA (Veterans Affairs; its VBA, the Veterans Benefits Administration); DoD (Defense); HHS (Health and Human Services); DOS (State); NARA (National Archives and Records Administration); USDA (Agriculture); DOT (Transportation); EEOC (Equal Employment Opportunity Commission).

Section 1:

  • Requests received rose from 560,978 in FY2008 to 1,707,413 in FY2025, 3.04 times as many. The growth was uneven: 858,952 in FY2019 and 838,164 in FY2021, then 2.04 times as many four years later.
  • Processing broadly kept pace (578,141 in FY2008, 1,635,171 in FY2025); processed ÷ received was 93.5% in FY2023 and 95.8% in FY2025 (Section 5 splits this).
  • Some agencies are missing in FY2008–09: 26 agencies have no FY2008 data and appear in FY2009–10; in FY2010 they account for 9.3% of government receipts and 12.1% outside the four units. The FY2008 starting level is therefore low and the 3.04 multiple somewhat overstated; from FY2010 it is 2.86, hence "nearly tripled" in the title.

Section 2:

  • Requests received at the four units rose from 110,388 to 962,166, 8.72 times as many; at the rest, from 450,590 to 745,247, 1.65 times. Of the 1,146,435 requests added government-wide over FY2008–25, 74.3% are at the four units.
  • The same from FY2010 (second table): all government 2.86 times, the four units 7.05 times, the rest 1.62 times; the four units' share of the increase is 74.4%. The central figure of this storyline does not depend on the starting year.
  • The four units' share of all requests rose from 19.7% in FY2008 to 47.3% in FY2019 and 58.7% in FY2024 (the peak), and was 56.4% in FY2025. All of DHS, which also includes PRIV and other components, rose from 19.4% to 59.7%.
  • Within the four units, mainly USCIS (third table): USCIS rose from 78,985 to 589,972, 60.0% of the four units' FY2008–25 increase and 61.3% of their FY2025 receipts; CBP accounts for 22.7% of the increase, EOIR 10.5% and ICE 6.8%. ICE received 62,238 in FY2025, fewer than its 64,231 in FY2019.
  • From FY2021 to FY2025 government receipts rose by 869,249, of which the four units account for 518,067 (59.6%), led by USCIS (+354,762). The five units adding the most (USCIS, CBP, PRIV, VBA and EOIR) account for 78.1%, but PRIV and VBA are not among the four units.

Government-wide request volume therefore mainly reflects the four immigration-related units, which is why the later storylines separate them first.

3. What about everyone else?¶

First, requests received outside the four units. The largest recent changes are at VBA and DHS PRIV, and PRIV's jump is in fact a transfer between two DHS components (second point below). So a second line also removes VBA and DHS's other components: how DHS's components should be grouped, and whether PRIV belongs with the immigration-related units, are both undecided, and removing all of them is the cleanest choice. Finally, how broad the rise is by agency, each agency against its own FY2014–19 average.

Section 3:

  • Outside the four units, roughly flat for more than a decade: 450,590 in FY2008 and 452,915 in FY2019, 411,481–470,719 each year in FY2010–19; 394,065 in FY2021, 548,380 in FY2023, already above the earlier maximum, and 745,247 in FY2025. Of the 351,182 added from FY2021 to FY2025, DHS's other components account for +100,705 and VBA for +89,244, 54.1% together; all other units +161,233 (second table). This is why the second line removes them.
  • PRIV's FY2019 jump is a transfer from CISA (first and third tables): CISA received 37,516 requests in FY2018 and 353 in FY2019, while PRIV went from 1,448 to 43,024. Pending requests line up too: PRIV started FY2019 with 2,589 pending, against 586 at its own FY2018 year end; adding CISA's 2,102 gives 2,688, close to that. Together the two form an almost continuous series: 8,480 in FY2014, 38,964 in FY2018, 43,377 in FY2019 and 148,873 in FY2025, 17.6 times as many over FY2014–25, with one down year (24,298 in FY2020). These requests rose steadily for more than a decade rather than jumping in FY2019.
  • VBA: 5,529 in FY2022, 61,658 in FY2023 and 100,897 in FY2025 (the same change behind VBA in Storylines 1 and 4).
  • Without VBA and DHS's other components: 394,268–444,654 each year in FY2010–19. FY2020–22 is a low (340,350, 322,865 and 326,473); FY2023, at 392,710, is still just below the earlier range; FY2024, at 417,789, is back within it; FY2025, at 484,098, is the highest of the 18 years, 49.9% above FY2021, 21.5% above FY2019 and 8.9% above the FY2010–19 maximum. Through FY2024 this is a recovery; only FY2025 is a new high.
  • Not driven by one agency: this line rose by 85,708 from FY2019 to FY2025; after also removing any one agency, the rise is still at least 64,000 (removing DOS, 16.4% above FY2019).
  • How broad depends on the comparison year (last table): against each agency's own FY2014–19 average, only 62 of 97 agencies were higher in FY2023; on the FY2024–25 average, 87 of 95 were higher, 20 of the 27 averaging at least 1,000 requests a year, and 8 of the 13 averaging at least 10,000 (the five not higher are NARA, SEC, SSA, USDA and VA without VBA).

6. The resulting story¶

In one sentence: government-wide FOIA requests received nearly tripled over FY2008–25 (3.04 times the FY2008 level, 2.86 times FY2010); 74.3% of the increase is at four immigration-related units (74.4% from FY2010), whose share of all requests rose from 19.7% to 58.7% in FY2024; requests at other units (without VBA and DHS's other components) were roughly flat for a decade, fell to a low in FY2020–22, recovered by FY2024, and were the highest of the 18 years only in FY2025.

Key points:

  1. Volume (Section 1): 560,978 in FY2008 to 1,707,413 in FY2025, 2.04 times as many in the last four years; 26 agencies are missing in FY2008, and from FY2010 the multiple is 2.86.
  2. Growth concentrated at the four immigration-related units (Section 2): 8.72 times as many, against 1.65 times elsewhere; the four units account for 74.3% of the increase (74.4% from FY2010), and USCIS alone for 60.0% of the four units' increase; of the 869,249 requests added in FY2021–25, 59.6% are at the four units.
  3. Everyone else (Section 3): without VBA and DHS's other components, 394,268–444,654 each year in FY2010–19, a low in FY2020–22, back within the earlier range in FY2024, and 484,098 in FY2025, 8.9% above the FY2010–19 maximum; on the FY2024–25 average, 87 of 95 agencies exceed their own FY2014–19 average, though larger agencies less uniformly: 20 of the 27 averaging at least 1,000 requests a year, and 8 of the 13 averaging at least 10,000. PRIV's FY2019 jump is a transfer from CISA.
  4. Highly concentrated (Section 4): DHS alone receives 59.7%, the top five agencies 84.1%.
  5. Processing not keeping up (Section 5): outside the four units, FY2025 processing was only 88.0% of receipts (see Storyline 4); and the perfected share of processed requests fell to 77.5% in FY2024.

What not to say:

  • Do not say FOIA requests surged across the board: 74.3% of the FY2008–25 increase is at four immigration-related units, and other units, apart from VBA and DHS's other components, were roughly flat for more than a decade, exceeding their earlier range only in FY2025.
  • Do not describe the rise at other units as a multi-year trend: without VBA and DHS's other components, only FY2025 exceeds the FY2010–19 range, and FY2025 has no following-year report yet.
  • Do not call the four units' requests "immigration requests": it is a grouping of units, not of request topics.
  • Do not read request counts as numbers of requesters: one requester can file many requests.
  • Do not treat FY2008 as covering all agencies: 26 agencies are missing that year.
  • Do not read PRIV's FY2019 rise as a jump in requests: the same requests were reported under CISA before; VBA's large rise from FY2023 also needs to be read on its own.
  • Do not state causes: the annual reports do not show why requests increased.

Leads to follow up separately:

  • CISA and PRIV: one stream of requests, 8,480 in FY2014 and 148,873 in FY2025, reported under CISA through FY2018 and under PRIV from FY2019. What these requests are, and whether the stream belongs with the immigration-related units, needs checking.
  • VBA: 61,658–100,897 a year from FY2023, the same change behind VBA's partial releases in Storyline 1 and the denominator in Storyline 4.
  • Why requests at other agencies recovered after the FY2020–22 low.
  • Outside the four units, FY2025 processing was only 88.0% of receipts.
  • More non-perfected requests: 59.1% of the FY2024 difference is at USCIS.

What the next storylines cover:

  • Storyline 1 (how requests are closed): outside DHS and NARA, the full-release share of processed requests fell by more than half over FY2008–25, mainly within agencies.
  • Storyline 3 (appeals): few requesters appeal, outcomes depend mainly on the agency, and DHS appeals surged in the last two years.
  • Storyline 4 (backlog): outside the four units, backlog has risen in stages relative to receipts, across most agencies.

Methods and remaining questions¶

  • Requests received include non-perfected requests (handbook Section V); counts are of requests, not requesters.
  • The de-duplication rule is the same as in the other storylines: agencies with components use component rows, agencies without them use the Agency Overall row, and Exclude and second-entry rows are dropped.
  • 26 agencies are missing in FY2008–09 (Section 1); FY2025 has no following-year report yet. The reports are self-reported.
  • The four units are a grouping of units; other agencies may also handle immigration-related requests.
  • Section 3 removes all of DHS's other components: requests have moved between DHS components (CISA to PRIV), and removing them all avoids deciding how DHS's components should be grouped.
  • Checks: the four units' share of the increase is checked against the starting year (74.3% from FY2008, 74.4% from FY2010) and split by unit (Section 2); the rise outside the four units is checked against removing single agencies and against different comparison years (Section 3). Shares and multiples are direct descriptions of the totals.
